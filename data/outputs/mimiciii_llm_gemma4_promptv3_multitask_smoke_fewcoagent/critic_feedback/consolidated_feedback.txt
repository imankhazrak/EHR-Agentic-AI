[Consolidated Criteria]
1. Prioritize explicit diagnoses over inferred risk factors when establishing current status.
2. Require a combination of risk factors or explicit diagnosis to support a positive prediction; avoid reliance on single, weak signals.
3. Recognize that multiple cardiovascular risk factors support a positive prediction for related complications.
4. Evaluate each condition independently; do not assume one condition implies another.
5. Recognize that a combination of known risk factors, even without an explicit diagnosis, can support a positive prediction for a related complication (addressing missed positives).
6. Distinguish between insufficient single signals and meaningful combinations of signals when assessing positive predictions.
7. Do not assume the absence of an explicit diagnosis means the condition is absent if strong risk factors are present.
8. Keep criteria generalizable and actionable, grounded solely in the provided samples.
9. Avoid overly strict rules requiring explicit diagnosis in all positive cases, allowing for inference from strong combinations of signals.
10. Do not introduce external medical assumptions.
